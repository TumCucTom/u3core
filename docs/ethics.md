

<h1>Ethics</h1>

<h3>Project background</h3>
<p><strong>Project goal :</strong>To develop a computer vision program that detects hazards such as fire smoke and oil spills and alerts relevant people. </p>
<p>End result : Ideally we will have a piece of software that can be given to clients that can help to manage health and safety in the workplace.</p>

<h3>Data collection for testing</h3>
<p><strong>Collection : </strong>Data will be collected primarily on the SEP testing day in April. We will have users interact with the software and enter their phone number and see if the software correctly locates the hazard and sends the SMS message</p>

<p><strong>Information about participants :</strong> Only personal information collected will be phone numbers for the alert SMS system which will only be used in processing and not saved</p>

<p>Informed consent : All participants will fill out an informed consent form prior to testing</p>

<h3>Possible ethical considerations</h3>
<p>The only personal information our project handles are phone numbers, for testing we will delete them immediately but in the actual software we will store them safely and securely. </p>


<h3>Route and justification</h3>

<h3>Data privacy and protection</h3>
<p>Because our software is not standalone, it is an integerated piece into a layer of other systems some of the data collected will depend on how the client uses it. However our client has assured us that all the data colleceted will be stored according to GDPR. </p>


<p>
 We've applied to use ethics route B for our user testing. In our testing we plan for users to use the software and give feedback on how reliable it is
in detecting hazards - specifically how accurate it is and whether there any false positives. They may be recorded if they are in front of the camera 
but none of the video will be saved during testing it will only be used for processing. As well as this their phone numbers may be taken down to test
the notification system but this will be deleted immediately after that specific user closes the program. Due to this I believe it would fall into
ethics route B.
</p>
